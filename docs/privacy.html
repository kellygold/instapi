InstaPi Privacy Policy

Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 24, 2026

The Short Version

InstaPi runs on your own hardware. It stores the photos you choose and settings on that device. Optional Google Photos access and sharing with your configured family frames send data only as needed for those features. The developer does not operate a central service that receives your photos or Google credentials.

How InstaPi Works

InstaPi is open-source software that runs on your own Raspberry Pi hardware. It displays locally stored photos while offline. Google Photos import is optional and requires an internet connection; family-frame sharing requires a connection to the frames you configure. There is no hosted InstaPi service. Here's what's important to understand:

  • No central server. There is no InstaPi server that processes your data. Your Raspberry Pi is both the client and the server.
  • You control photo storage and sharing. Selected photos are downloaded from Google to your Raspberry Pi and stored locally. If you enable family-frame sharing, photos and their associated metadata are transferred to the frames you configure. Remote access may route traffic through a tunnelling service you choose.
  • Open source. You can verify every claim in this policy by reviewing the source code.

Google Account Access

InstaPi uses Google OAuth to let you sign in with your Google account and select photos. Here's exactly what happens:

  • Minimal permissions. InstaPi requests only the photospicker.mediaitems.readonly scope, which allows you to pick specific photos to share. It cannot browse your full Google Photos library or upload, edit or delete items in Google Photos.
  • Tokens stay on your device. InstaPi stores the OAuth access token and Picker session state in its local SQLite database. The token is sent to Google to authorize Picker requests. Protect access to your device, its database and backups. The developer does not receive these tokens.
  • No token storage by us. The InstaPi developer has no server that receives, processes, or stores your Google credentials. The OAuth flow happens directly between your Pi and Google.
  • Revoke anytime. You can revoke InstaPi's access to your Google account at any time from your Google Account settings.

Data We Do Not Collect

The InstaPi developer does not receive the following through a central application service. Your own installation processes photos, local settings and authentication data to operate the features described above:

  • Personal information (name, email, phone number)
  • Usage analytics or telemetry
  • Device identifiers or IP addresses
  • Cookies or tracking data
  • Photos or photo metadata
  • Google account credentials or tokens
  • Location data

Local Data Storage

On your Raspberry Pi, InstaPi stores the following locally:

  • Downloaded photos in the Pi's local filesystem for display on your frame.
  • Settings and photo metadata, including slideshow preferences, filenames, uploader labels and family-frame synchronization state, in a local SQLite database.
  • Authentication and access settings, including Google access tokens, Picker session state and frame-sharing tokens, on your device. The admin interface uses a session cookie to keep you signed in.

All of this data is stored on your hardware, under your control. You can remove photos through the admin interface or erase local application data on your device. Revoking Google access stops future authorized requests; it does not delete photos already downloaded or copies on other configured frames. Remove those copies separately when no longer wanted.

Third-Party Services

Depending on the features you enable, your installation can interact with these services:

  • Google Photos Picker API — Used to let you select photos from your Google Photos library. This interaction is governed by Google's Privacy Policy. InstaPi uses the Picker API's limited access scope, meaning it can only access photos you explicitly select.
  • Optional remote-access providers — A service such as ngrok can route traffic to your frame when you configure remote access. That provider processes traffic and connection information according to its own policies.
  • Website assets — This public site loads fonts and styling from Google Fonts and Tailwind CDN. Those providers receive ordinary browser requests, which can include your IP address.

Google API Data Use

InstaPi uses Google Photos data to provide the photo-selection and display features described here, including sharing to frames you configure. Google user data is not sold, used for advertising, or used to train generalized AI models. InstaPi's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

How Google User Data Is Protected

Google user data handled by InstaPi is the photos and videos you select in the Google Photos Picker, their filenames and related metadata, and the access token and Picker session details Google issues for that selection. InstaPi protects it in these ways:

  • Encrypted between your device and Google. Sign-in, Picker requests and photo downloads go directly from your device to Google over HTTPS.
  • Google handles sign-in. You sign in on Google's own pages. InstaPi never sees or stores your Google password. The sign-in exchange uses PKCE and a one-time state value, so an authorization code cannot be redeemed without the secret held on your device.
  • Short-lived access only. InstaPi stores only the short-lived access token Google issues for the Picker. It does not store a refresh token, so it cannot keep accessing Google Photos after that token expires; you sign in again to add more photos.
  • Narrowest available permission. The Picker scope exposes only the items you explicitly select. InstaPi cannot browse, search, upload to, edit or delete anything in your Google Photos library.
  • The token stays on your frame. It is stored in that frame's local database and is never sent to the developer or to other frames. There is no InstaPi server.
  • Password-protected administration. Deleting photos, changing settings and Factory Reset through the admin interface require signing in with the device's own account password, and repeated failed sign-in attempts are temporarily locked out. A frame you have connected for sharing can also delete photos using its sharing token.

What is not protected by a password. InstaPi is a photo frame, so the slideshow and the photos it displays are served to anyone who can reach the device on your network without signing in. Treat every photo you add as visible to people on that network. If you set up remote access through a tunnelling service, the same applies to anyone who has that address. Sharing with other frames sends your selected photos and their metadata to the frames you configure, over the connection you configure; that connection is encrypted only if you use an HTTPS address. Because InstaPi runs on your own hardware, you are responsible for the security of the device, its network and any remote access you set up.

Retention and Deletion of Google User Data

The InstaPi developer retains no Google user data, because none is ever sent to the developer. On your own device:

  • Selected photos are kept until you delete them. You can delete individual photos in the admin interface. Copies already synced to other frames you configured are removed the next time those frames sync successfully; a frame that is no longer connected or reachable keeps its copies until you delete them or reset it.
  • The Google access token stays in the local database until your next successful Google sign-in replaces it or you run Factory Reset. It stops working when it expires; Google access tokens typically last about an hour.
  • Picker session details stay in the local database until a new Picker session replaces them, a new Google sign-in clears them, or you run Factory Reset.
  • Sign-in records in device logs. The device's web server log records the address of each request, which includes the one-time authorization code Google returns at sign-in. That code is used once, immediately, and cannot be redeemed again. The log stays on your device.
  • Factory Reset in the admin interface deletes the downloaded photos on that device and clears its stored settings, including the Google access token and Picker session details. Run it when no photo import or frame sync is in progress, and reset other frames separately.
  • Revoking access from your Google Account settings prevents any further authorized access to Google Photos. It does not delete the stored token or photos already downloaded. Factory Reset removes the stored token and local photos; downloaded photos can also be deleted individually in the admin interface.

If you need help removing your data, email hello@instapi.dev.

Children's Privacy

The developer does not operate user accounts or a central photo collection service. Frame owners are responsible for choosing appropriate photos and access settings, including when photos include children or frames are shared with family members.
